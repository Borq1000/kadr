This is the same machine as the M0 and M1 documents. FFmpeg is not involved: frames are synthetic and in memory, so the numbers are the cost of compositing alone (no decode, no colour conversion, no upload). Command: `cargo run --release -p kadr-bench -- render`. Other agents were compiling on the machine during the session (see "Noise" below).

## 2. Method

- **Frames**, built once per output size before timing: two opaque 1920×1080 (or 3840×2160) "video" frames (`ColorInfo::WORKING_SDR` with `alpha: Opaque`; diagonal gradients plus xorshift noise) and one 512×512 straight-alpha logo (a disc with a soft edge over the outer 15 %).
- **Output** = canvas = the frame size, `RenderQuality::Export`: 1920×1080 for the 1080p rows, 3840×2160 for the 4K rows. Background opaque black.
- **Rows** (one output size each):
  - `1 layer`: one full-frame opaque layer (identity placement, opacity 1, Normal, no effects): the row-copy fast path.
  - `1 layer scaled`: the same layer with scale 0.98, rotation 0: the general per-pixel path (bilinear sampling, edge coverage, 1 % black margin).
  - `2 layers`: the full-frame layer plus a picture-in-picture of the second video frame at scale 0.3, rotated 5°, in the lower right.
  - `3 layers`: the same plus the logo at scale 0.5 in the top-left corner.
  - `transition`: a single `Dissolve` transition layer at progress 0.5 whose `from` and `to` are two full-frame video layers; nothing else in the scene.
- **Timing**: per row, 5 warm-up renders, then 120 timed renders at 1080p and 60 at 4K, each into the same output buffer (`CpuTarget::packed` over a `Vec` allocated once). One `CpuRenderer` per output size. Each sample is the wall time of one `render` call. p50, p90 and max come from `Stats::of`.
- **Allocations**: `frame_allocs_per_frame` = (counting allocator's count of allocations ≥ 1 MiB after the timed renders − before) / number of timed renders, so it covers the timed renders only. `pool_allocs_delta` is the change of `CpuRenderer::pool_allocations()` over the same renders (the transition buffers come from the renderer's `FramePool`).
- The unit test `render_bench::tests::bench_scenes_have_the_advertised_layers` checks that each row has the advertised number of top-level layers and the right content kinds (media layers, or one transition layer holding two media layers), so no row silently renders less than it says.

## 4. Verdict on the M2 exit criterion

Criterion: "bench: 1080p and 4K render ms for 1/2/3 layers and a transition-only row; 0 frame-sized allocations per frame after warm-up".

- **Rows: met.** Both sizes have `1 layer`, `2 layers`, `3 layers` and a transition-only row, plus a `1 layer scaled` row that isolates the general path from the fast path.
- **Frame-sized allocations: met.** 0 per frame in every one of the 10 rows in all three runs, and the renderer's own pool allocations do not change after warm-up (delta 0). The transition row is the one that needs scratch buffers (the `from` and `to` buffers), and they are reused from the pool. The output buffer is the caller's, so the renderer allocates none. The counting allocator counts allocations of at least 1 MiB in the whole process, so this also covers rayon and anything else in the timed region.

Observations, without a target attached (the stage sets none):

- The fast path is a memory copy: 0.70 ms at 1080p and 2.42 ms at 4K (8.3 MB and 33.2 MB per frame).
- The general path is what costs: a single scaled layer takes 7.56 ms at 1080p and 30.65 ms at 4K, which is about 4.05 times the pixel count and 4.05 times the time. `2 layers` and `3 layers` are cheap (1.79 to 2.24 ms at 1080p, 6.75 to 6.80 ms at 4K) because the bottom layer is a fast-path copy and the top layers cover a small area (PiP at 0.3, logo at 0.5 of 512 px).
- A dissolve between two full-frame layers costs 4.87 ms at 1080p and 17.26 ms at 4K: two full-frame layers drawn into transition buffers plus the mix.
- At 1080p every row is under 8 ms at p50 and under 9 ms at p90, i.e. within a 60 fps frame budget of 16.7 ms. At 4K the rows with a full-frame general-path layer (`1 layer scaled` at 30.65 ms, `transition` at 17.26 ms) are above 16.7 ms, and `1 layer scaled` at p90 (34.71 ms) is above a 30 fps budget of 33.3 ms. Export is not real-time so this does not block M2, but it is the first place to look if 4K editing on the CPU path matters.
- These are CPU renders of ready pixels. A real frame also pays for decode and YUV to RGB conversion, which are not part of this bench.

## 5. Tests

`cargo test -p kadr-render` (dev profile with the workspace's optimisation settings):

| target | passed | failed |
|---|---:|---:|
| unit tests (`src/lib.rs`: `CpuRenderer`, cost, API) | 21 | 0 |
| golden (`tests/golden.rs`) | 11 | 0 |
| parity (`tests/reference_parity.rs`) | 12 | 0 |
| doc tests | 0 | 0 |

The **parity** target holds the independent reference renderer's checks. It renders seeded pseudo-random scenes (1 to 4 layers with random placement, rotation, scale, anchor, crop, opacity, blend mode and colour adjust; straight, opaque and premultiplied frames of random sizes; solids; missing inputs; transitions of all three kinds with nested layers; odd output sizes and letterboxed outputs) with both `CpuRenderer` and a naive single-threaded f64 renderer written from the contract text, and compares every channel of every pixel. 400 scenes run in every `cargo test`; the maximum difference is 1 LSB at pixels that are not rounding ties (at a tie, where an f32 and an f64 renderer may round `k + 0.5` in different directions, the allowed difference is widened as the test documents). 6000 scenes were checked once with `PARITY_SCENES=6000`, outside the regular test run. The other 11 tests in the target are hand-computed sanity checks (for example 50 % red over blue is (128, 0, 128), saturation 0 gives Rec.709 grey, letterbox margins are opaque black).

The bench crate's own tests: `cargo test -p kadr-bench` runs 12 tests, all passing, three of them new (`bench_scenes_have_the_advertised_layers`, `geometry_and_frame_kinds_match_the_row_names`, `a_small_run_reports_every_metric_of_every_row`).
