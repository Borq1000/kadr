## 5. Live preview (headless Kadr)

Headless Kadr driven through kadr-mcp, one clip on the timeline, preview quality ½, 20 s of playback (600 frames at 30 fps), then 10 seeks. Statistics come from `get_perf`. Milliseconds unless stated.

How to read the columns:

- `composite_ms` is **n/a (legacy has no compositor)**. The raw output prints 0.00 for it; that is an absent stage, not a 0 ms measurement.
- `present_ms` is UI-thread upload/set time (SharedPixelBuffer copy plus Slint property set). It is not the time until the frame is on screen.
- In playback, `decode_ms` is the time to read one frame from an already-running FFmpeg pipe (read-ahead). It is not the true decode cost; the decode cost is in section 3. Playback `total_ms` excludes the wait on the audio clock.
- `mb_copied_per_frame` is MiB (mebibytes), at ½ quality: 1080p half is 960x540 RGBA = 1.98 MiB, 4K half is 1920x1080 RGBA = 7.91 MiB.
- Frame allocations and copies per frame are frame-sized ones on the preview path (decode buffer plus the copy into the UI-thread pixel buffer).

|---|---:|---:|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| h264_1080p30 | 600 | 6 | 1.30 / 1.70 | 0.88 / 1.24 | 0.39 / 0.50 | 1.99 | 0.99 | 1.96 |
| h264_2160p30 | 600 | 11 | 5.51 / 7.42 | 3.96 / 5.77 | 1.55 / 1.91 | 1.98 | 0.98 | 7.76 |

Dropped frames over the 20 s run: 1.0 % (h264 1080p), 1.8 % (h264 4K), 1.5 % (hevc 4K). Worst single frames: decode max 2.74 / 353.26 / 304.78 ms, total max 3.10 / 11.85 / 13.11 ms (the 4K decode maxima of 300 to 350 ms were each observed once in these runs (this says nothing about how often they occur in general); the p99 decode is 8.08 ms for h264 4K and 6.91 ms for hevc 4K).

### Seek (10 seeks)

| clip | seek p50 | seek p90 | seek max | total p50 | decode p50 | present p50 | frame allocs / frame | frame copies / frame | MiB copied / frame |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| h264_1080p30 | 357.57 | 404.42 | 408.29 | 357.48 | 357.12 | 0.35 | 2.00 | 1.00 | 1.98 |

The h264 4K seek run had 11 decoded frames and 1 dropped frame for 10 seeks, and a seek max of 1324.34 ms while its total and decode maxima are 759.03 and 757.70 ms. This is reported as measured; it happened in one sample of 10 in this run and is not explained by decode time (a dropped or superseded request is the likely cause). The 15-sample bench seek of the same clip (section 2) shows max 748.47 ms, so it did not show up there. Seek in the live editor is of the same order as the bench seek (1080p: live p50 357.57 ms against bench 315.97 ms; 4K: about 0.5 to 0.6 s in both), because both start a new FFmpeg process.

## 6. Legacy export

Scenario: 1080p30, 20 s, 10 cuts, 2 dissolves, Balanced preset.

| scenario | fps |
|---|---:|
| export_legacy | 133.30 |

The export was measured with the fixes from commits f626fa1 and b33c198. On this branch a real export bug was found while building this scenario: two or more plain cuts before a transition failed with an xfade time-base error (FFmpeg rejected the concat output time base 1/1000000 next to 1/fps), with FFmpeg 8.0.1 (the version measured). The concat output time base is now normalised before xfade, with a regression test. The number above is therefore the export with the fix in place.

## 7. Targets for the new pipeline

- M3: seek latency beats section 2 on every row (same clips, same sizes). For each row the new pipeline's p50 AND p90 must be strictly below the section 2 values below; max is informational only (15 samples, noisy) and is reported alongside.
  - h264_1080p30 full: p50 < 315.97 ms, p90 < 376.40 ms
  - h264_1080p30 half: p50 < 316.89 ms, p90 < 374.68 ms
  - h264_2160p30 full: p50 < 572.69 ms, p90 < 743.40 ms
  - h264_2160p30 half: p50 < 534.67 ms, p90 < 696.73 ms
  - hevc_2160p30 full: p50 < 485.17 ms, p90 < 569.54 ms
  - hevc_2160p30 half: p50 < 447.11 ms, p90 < 524.49 ms
- M2/M3: steady-state frame-sized allocations per frame = 0 (versus 1.00 in section 3 and about 2 in section 5).
- M4: UI-thread frame copies per frame = 0 (versus about 1 in section 5, 1.98 MiB at 1080p half and about 7.8 MiB at 4K half).
- M4: dropped frames at most 1 % over 60 s (the legacy 20 s runs show 1.0 % to 1.8 %).
- M5: export fps compared to section 6 (133.30 fps for the 1080p30 Balanced scenario).

## 8. Measurement setup for M4

- The M4 drop target (section 7) is measured with `kadr-bench live --seconds 60`, preview quality ½, on the same clips as section 5 (`h264_1080p30`, `h264_2160p30`, `hevc_2160p30`), and additionally with 3-layer runs.
- The legacy 1080p row of section 5 was recorded over 20 s. It will be re-measured over 60 s in M4 so the drop comparison is like-for-like.
