## 2. Method

`export_bench.rs` builds the scenarios as `Project`s and exports them twice: once on the new pipeline (`kadr_playback::export::export` over `ProjectScenes` + `FfmpegDecoders`, exactly what the app builds in `export_ui::encode_job`: even size, `frames = rate.time_to_frame_round(total)`, crf 21 preset "fast") and once on the legacy FFmpeg-graph exporter (`FfmpegCli::export` with the plan `build_export_plan` mirrors). fps rows run 3 exports each; the median run is reported (min/max shown). The legacy M0 row runs in the same process, on the same media, for a same-conditions comparison.

All media is the M0 bench media (`media::ensure`, h264, BT.709-tagged, 2 s GOP) plus a generated `logo512.png` (512×512 RGBA, soft-edged disc, straight alpha). No audio in the fps and PSNR scenarios, as in M0 §6.

- **Row a (M0 scenario)**: 10 × 2 s segments of `h264_1080p30` from source offsets `i·5 s`, 0.5 s dissolves into segments 3 and 7, 1920×1080@30, crf 21 "fast" — the timeline of M0 §6 as a project.
- **Row b (4K)**: 4 × 2.5 s segments (3 cuts) of `h264_2160p30`, output 3840×2160.
- **Row c (3 layers)**: row a's timeline, plus the 4K clip as a picture-in-picture on V2 (scale 0.3, offset, rotated 5°) and the logo on V3.
- **Diag rows** isolate the pipeline: one full-frame 1080p clip (20 s, no cuts), the same plus the small logo, and both again with a `NullFactory` encoder that drops the frames (measures decode + resolve + render + hand-off without x264).

Render p50/p90, decode wait (`Resolver::prepare`), encoder wait (free-buffer + send back-pressure) and writer time come from `ExportStats`. `ExportStats` gained `layers_drawn` / `fast_paths` (sums of `RenderStats` over all frames) for this investigation.

## 4. Fast-path investigation

The smoke report of render p50 6.67 ms for a full-frame layer + small logo was reproducible (diag rows: 8.56 ms p50). Findings, in order:

1. **The fast path is hit.** With `RenderStats` surfaced through `ExportStats`: the single-clip diag row reports 600 of 600 layers via the row-copy path; the M0 scenario reports 570/600 — the 30 missing are the frames inside the two 0.5 s dissolves, which composite through the transition path by design. The 3-layer row reports 570/1800: the base layer behaves like row a, the rotated PiP and the alpha logo sample per-pixel by design. Nothing about export decouples the renderer from its fast path.
2. **Decode size, alpha and placement are correct.** A new integration test (`a_full_frame_opaque_layer_is_drawn_by_the_fast_path` in `crates/playback/tests/export.rs`) exports a full-canvas opaque fake clip and asserts every frame is drawn by the fast path — the whole export path (scene → resolver decode size → placement → renderer) keeps the identity case intact.
3. **The time is the copy itself plus x264 contention.** With frames dropped (no encoder process at all), the same render is 2.36 ms p50 and the pipeline sustains 174 fps — decode wait included. With x264 "fast" running, render p50 rises to ~8.6–10.2 ms and the export lands at 60–72 fps: the 12-thread rayon render and the multi-threaded encoder compete for cores and memory bandwidth. `RAYON_NUM_THREADS=1` (probe) gave a slightly *better* end-to-end fps (112 vs 107) with the same render p50, confirming the contention reading. The row copy is two full-frame memory passes (base fill + copy, ~32 MiB at 1080p); at this machine's bandwidth that is several ms on its own.
4. **Not decode, not the encoder pipe.** Decode wait is 0.08–1.8 s per 600-frame export and encoder wait 0.0–0.4 s; the writer thread's total `write_frame` time (2–3 s of ~10 s) is the pipe into x264, overlapped with rendering by the double buffer.

**Verdict:** the fast path works in export; the fps gap to legacy is x264 + renderer CPU contention (legacy feeds x264 from ffmpeg's own C graph, no Rust-side rayon render). Possible follow-ups (not done here): skipping the base fill when one opaque layer covers the canvas, and capping the render pool's threads during export. Numbers to re-take on a quiet machine in M6.

## 6. A/V sync, 20 minutes (exit: ±1 frame)

**Source**: `avsync_1200s_2997_720p.mp4` in `bench-media` (20 min, 1280×720, 30000/1001). `avsync::generate_source` was changed so the click for second `s` starts on the flash frame's first sample (`round(ceil(s·num/den)·den·48000/num)` — at 29.97 e.g. sample 48 048 for s = 1), so the source itself has ~0 offset instead of up to 0.99 frame; a unit test pins the sample math and the self-test now asserts |offset| < 0.5 ms.

**Timeline** (`av_plan`, unit-tested): V1 + A1 linked clips of that source, 52 cuts every 23 s at mid-second positions (12.5 s + 23 s·k, never on a whole second), each clip taking its source from a whole-frame offset nearest a whole second (so whole-second flashes stay within a frame of whole timeline seconds, far from the cuts); 13 cross-dissolves of 8 frames centred on every 4th cut (they sit between flashes); audio clips follow the same cuts and source ranges, so flash and click move together by construction. Export on the new pipeline with audio (720p): 174.73 fps, 205.8 s.

| flashes | clicks | pairs | unmatched flashes | unmatched clicks | max \|offset\| | mean offset |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1200 | 1199 | 1199 | 1 | 0 | **0.02 ms (0.00 frames)** | −0.00 ms |

**Verdict: PASS with one finding.** Every one of the 1199 pairs is within 0.02 ms — three orders of magnitude inside the ±1 frame (33.4 ms) limit, cuts and dissolves included. The one unmatched flash is the flash at t = 0: **the click at second 0 is missing from the export**. Cause, reproduced with a minimal ffmpeg command: the audio graph (`crates/media/src/export.rs`, `audio_graph`, shared by the legacy path and the new encoder job) always emits an input-side `-ss 0.000000` per clip; FFmpeg's input seek on an AAC/MP4 source drops the priming samples of the first AAC granule (~215 samples ≈ 4.5 ms here), which silences the 5 ms click sitting exactly at sample 0 (the same command without `-ss` keeps it). Clicks at every other second — including the first click of every *other* clip, whose `-ss` target is mid-second — are all within 0.02 ms. Fix suggestion (not applied, file is outside this task's scope): omit `-ss` when `source_start` is zero, or seek on the output side.

## 8. Addendum: quiet-machine re-run and the render-pool experiment

Re-run 2026-09-30 with nothing else running (no builds, no agents), after `render/cpu` started skipping the canvas base fill under a full-canvas opaque first layer. Same scenarios, median of 3 runs each.

| scenario | new, global pool | new, render pool capped to cores/2 |

| 1080p 3 layers | **70.80 fps** (11.55 ms) | 69.66 fps (11.96 ms) |

The capped pool was no faster anywhere, so it was not kept. The legacy export in the same runs measured 67.47 and 122.41 fps (its three-run spread was 65–141 fps: the FFmpeg graph is sensitive to what else runs), so the M0 figure (133.30 fps) stays the reference for legacy; the new pipeline's 99 fps on the M0 scenario is within the range legacy showed in these runs, and the final numbers are taken again in M6.
